Open box vs refurbished vs new: what you actually get

Published November 18, 2026 · Deal Dude team

Retailers use "open box", "refurbished", "renewed" and "used" in slightly different ways. The labels matter because they affect condition, warranty and your right to return the item. Here is what each one usually means.

At a glance

What it isSealed, never soldReturned, display or damaged-packaging units, inspected by the storeUsed or defective units repaired and tested

Open box

An open-box item was sold and returned, used as a display model, or had its packaging damaged. The store inspects it before reselling. Many retailers grade open-box stock, with labels like "Excellent", "Good" or "Fair", based on cosmetic condition and missing accessories. Read the grade description on the listing, because there is no industry standard.

Manufacturer vs seller refurbished

Manufacturer refurbished (sometimes "certified refurbished") means the original brand restored the item, typically with genuine parts, new packaging and a manufacturer warranty. Seller refurbished (including programs like "renewed") means a third party did the work. Quality can be excellent, but it depends on that seller, and so does the warranty.

Rule of thumb: If the price gap between open box and manufacturer refurbished is small, open box is usually the safer choice. If the gap is big and the warranty is solid, refurbished can be the better deal.

Before you buy

  • Who provides the warranty, and for how long?
  • Is the return window the same as for new items?
  • Is the seller the retailer itself or a marketplace seller?
  • For laptops and phones: is the battery new or tested, and is the device unlocked?
