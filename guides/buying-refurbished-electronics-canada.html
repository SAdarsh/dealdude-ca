Buying refurbished electronics in Canada: a checklist

Published November 18, 2026 · Deals Dude team

Refurbished electronics can save you a lot of money, especially laptops, phones, tablets and game consoles. Use this checklist to make sure the savings are real.

The checklist

  1. Who refurbished it? The manufacturer, the retailer, or a third-party seller?
  2. What is the warranty? How long it lasts, who honours it, and whether it covers parts and labour.
  3. What is the return window? It should give you time to test everything.
  4. What condition grade is it? Read the seller's definition; there is no universal standard.
  5. Battery health. For phones and laptops, ask whether the battery was replaced or what capacity it retains.
  6. Exact model and specs. Check the processor, RAM, storage and screen against the model number.
  7. Is it unlocked and clean? Phones should be carrier-unlocked if listed that way. Every device should be free of the previous owner's accounts and activation locks.
  8. What's in the box? Charger, cables and accessories may be generic or missing.
  9. Is it a Canadian model? Keyboard layout, plug type and warranty coverage can differ for imported units.
  10. How does the price compare? Check new and open-box prices too. Sometimes a sale on a new unit beats the refurbished price.

When you get it

  • Test every port, button, camera, speaker and microphone in the first few days.
  • Check for software updates and confirm the device registers for warranty, if applicable.
  • Keep the packaging until the return window closes.
Refurbished is also a greener choice: it keeps working devices out of landfill and reduces demand for new manufacturing.
