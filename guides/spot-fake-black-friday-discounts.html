How to spot a fake Black Friday discount

Published November 18, 2026 · Deals Dude team

Most Black Friday discounts are genuine, but some are less impressive than they look. A few quick checks will tell you which is which.

1. The inflated "was" price

A "save 50%" claim is only meaningful if the item really sold at the higher price. Canada's Competition Act limits how retailers may advertise "regular" or "ordinary" prices, but the easiest protection is your own record. Check the price a few weeks before the sale, or look at a price history if one is available.

2. Similar, but not the same, model

Some doorbuster products are special versions with a different model number than the well-reviewed one, and slightly different specs. Compare model numbers exactly, not just the product name.

3. Fees that appear at checkout

Shipping, environmental handling fees and marketplace seller charges can wipe out a small discount. Compare the price you would actually pay before tax.

4. Bundles you did not want

A bundle with accessories can look like great value, but only if you would have bought those accessories anyway. Price the main item on its own.

5. Countdown pressure

"Only 2 left" and countdown timers push quick decisions. If the item is on your list and the price is genuinely good, buy it. If not, the next sale is never far away.

Quick test: Would you pay this price for this exact item on a normal Tuesday? If yes, it is a good deal. If you are only tempted by the percentage, skip it.

Don't forget open box

Sometimes the best "Black Friday deal" is not on the flyer at all. It is an open-box unit of the same model, already cheaper than the sale price of a new one.
